02 · RF vs the foundation model

On rivers they tie. The arroyo is where they part.

Across the four river-corridor reaches, a Random Forest and a fine-tuned foundation model transfer about equally — roughly 0.80 AUC apiece. The split shows up on the one morphology the RF had no sibling to learn from: a dry arroyo.

That single number — the arroyo the per-pixel RF can't generalise to — is what the foundation model's spatial context buys, and why it ships for the invasive task. For plain riparian extent, RF is as good and free of a GPU.

03 · The beetle that didn't break it

A pre-registered control vetoed our own hypothesis

We predicted a today-trained tamarisk classifier would invert before the tamarisk beetle arrived (~2004–07), when the greenness signal flips. It didn't — tamarisk-vs-native holds across the eras. And the negative control (Russian olive, which the beetle doesn't touch) moved seven times more than the tamarisk "signal," so the data simply can't resolve a beetle effect. Declaring the control in advance let it kill the result instead of us over-reading noise.

04 · How far back can we see?

A trajectory we chose not to claim

We tried to reconstruct invasive extent back to 1990 on the Landsat archive. The honest answer is a documented negative: multi-year window composites, spectral indices and relative radiometric normalisation stabilised the estimate across 2000–2010, but before ~2000 — pure Landsat-5 TM — single-year swings of ~1 percentage point swamp any trend. So there is no over-time trajectory claim here, only the reliable present-day product above.

Why this is in the story: the discipline that makes the 23% trustworthy is the same one that makes us not draw a 1990→2020 line. A result you can't reproduce under a reasonable change of recipe isn't a result yet.

The honest positioning

  • Riparian extent is already solved for this basin (CO-RIP, κ 0.80) — the RF reproduces it; that's the control, not the contribution.
  • The invasive discriminator is phenology / SWIR, not NDVI — and it looks beetle-robust, though the in-basin sample is too small to prove it.
  • The foundation model earns its keep on under-represented morphology (the arroyo), not on the river corridors where RF already transfers.
  • What we won't claim: a pre-2000 trajectory. What is real: a present-day, corridor-masked, native-vs-invasive product.
